# From Implementator to Manager


One of the biggest contradictions I had in my career when I was young was watching people managing companies or teams with little or none technical skills. I struggeld understanding why a manager could lead a team without coding or not at least in recent years. Take for instance a Proyect Manager, they lead people but they never code.

Another part was the admiration, and curiosity, I have for Vice Presidents or C-level people. I always wondered how their meetings could be like? What could they be talking about in such level meetings with other VPs? What do they have to study or to do to become one? But must importantly, do I have what it takes to become one?

I remember, and I qoute, something a newly promoter CEO told us in an Ask-Me-Anything talk:

**We have lots of VPs, will they all become CEO? Of course not!**

So the competition is hard! Probably harder at C-level than in the A-level level. A senior engineer competes with other seniors for a position in a project, but normally (or hopefully) there are multiple projects. Therefore multiple chances. 

We are familiar with the seniority pyramid. Junior -> Semi Sr -> Senior -> Team Lead -> B-track.

However, for a software engineer, people management only becomes real after becoming a Team Lead - Level 4 in our pyramid. This is the same for the Business Analyst who becomes a Project Manager at that level.

But, what happens at B-track?

Well you have to make a decision, one of the most difficult ones I'd say. 

**You have to chooose between deepening in the technical skills or start taking a managerial role.**

Even in the architecture career there's a limit of how much you can grow and how much complexity a system can have. Even the most complicated project is broken down in pieces a team of professionals can handle.

One of the biggest progressions in my career was passing from Solution Architecture to Talent Manager. The TM role is an extra mile, I am still accountable for the architecture in my projects, I do have to join Daily Stand Ups and drawing diagrams as usual.

**Being a TM requires a complete different mindset, the problems are not technical but human.**

Most people don't to enroll on an extra mile activity to instead work on their individual careers or use the time for anything else. That's also fine, not everyone has the human skills to make others grow, and it's not their fault not to have them but it's a decision not to nurturing them.
